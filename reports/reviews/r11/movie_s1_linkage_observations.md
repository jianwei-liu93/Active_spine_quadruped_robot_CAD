# r11 Movie S1 linkage and assembly observations

## Scope and evidence

This review covers only linkage-bar morphology, joint assembly, and the nearby central-frame/torso interface visible in the r11 Movie S1 extracts. It is a visual source review, not a CAD execution, dimensioned reconstruction, kinematic remapping, or fabrication assessment. ## Comparison with the r10 link model

The r10 source-fidelity record maps L2 to G–B, L3 to A–B, L4 to B–C, and L5 to C–D; E–D remains within the moving torso bodies. It describes L2/L3/L5 as plain capsule plates and L4 as a broad through-relief profile. Those roles and the four-long-versus-short/plain distinction remain a useful working interpretation, but the new sequence adds assembly evidence and narrows the profile conclusions.

The four r10 L4 instances have a 6 mm-wide, 1.2 mm-thick assumed plate, Ø4.05 mm assumed bores, and a 3.2 mm rounded-rectangle through-relief. The video supports a conspicuously relieved long member with broad rails, so the r10 change from a narrow slot toward a broad relief is directionally supported. The source appearance also has a more sculpted margin/shoulder at the relief and eye region than the current uniform flat plate render. The views do not tell whether the center is cut through or pocketed, and cannot establish the relief width, remaining rail thickness, local thickness, or end-boss geometry. Treat every r10 value as `DESIGN_ASSUMPTION`; do not cite the video as validating 3.2 mm, 1.2 mm, Ø4.05 mm, or the 5.5 mm relief inset.

L2/L3/L5 still read broadly as plate-like links with rounded ends, so retaining a simpler profile family is reasonable. However, the visible narrow bands/grooves on some short members mean “plain” should mean no large through-relief, not necessarily a featureless constant-section capsule. The effect could be a shallow face recess, raised edge web, chamfer, or an overlapping neighboring surface. The frame set is not sufficient to assign this detail to a specific S9 segment; do not add the same relief to every short link by analogy.

The sharpest r10 assembly discrepancy is the joint representation. The movie explicitly stages long transverse shafts after the planar bars have been placed, then shows larger shoulders/collars and broad exposed caps. The rods are therefore visually distinct from cross-axis hardware. A source-faithful assembly should make the axial members, their shoulders, and outer capture visually legible as separate hardware around the plate links. The apparent shoulder spans and end fittings vary by joint. r10's central E stack is a separate design assumption and is not evidence that A/B/C share its pin, washer, bearing, or retainer arrangement.

The film suggests paired axial layers and some locally fork-like or cheek-straddled connections, but no clean frame exposes an isolated bar end. A double edge can be a fork integrated into a link, two nearby planar link plates, a spacer/collar, or a pin shoulder. No named L2/L3/L4/L5 end can yet be asserted to be a manufactured clevis fork. Likewise, the frames do not prove a single continuous shaft at every corresponding bilateral station; the visible long shafts and the insertion sequence support a shared transverse span at several stations, but exact shaft-to-station mapping needs an axial or rear view.

At the center, the visible dark cradle/crossmember is fixed while the outer slotted torso-side plate changes pose. The output connection appears to include a broad round hub/flange and separate plate-like interface surfaces around the torso output. This is compatible with a moving torso/output carrier and fixed central support; it does not justify restoring a separate E–D rod, assigning the red schematic bar to a physical connection, or specifying the hidden bearing/clevis stack. The r10 ownership rule—E–D carried by the torso body—should remain unless better direct evidence contradicts it.

## What the evidence supports changing, and what it does not

1. **Keep the S9 link-center graph, link ownership, and instance separation.** The movie confirms several separate planar rods around the frame but does not relabel pivots. Keep L2=G–B, L3=A–B, L4=B–C, L5=C–D and the torso-carried E–D connection; do not add a physical L6 or convert red overlay geometry into bars.
2. **Retain a separate relieved L4 family, then improve its section description.** Preserve the broad central relief category and broad rails. Revisit whether the CAD should depict a shallow recessed web, a raised rim, or locally stepped shoulders at the ends when a clear axial/rear view is available. Current r10 straight-through cut is a plausible profile interpretation, not a confirmed source cross-section.
3. **Audit all short-bar faces before freezing their appearance.** Keep L2/L3/L5 without a large through-slot for now. Compare their face margins and eye transitions against the narrow bands in 56–57 s; only add relief where a clean view identifies it on a specific physical bar. Do not invent a generic bevel, fork, or thickness value.
4. **Show cross-axis hardware as separate assembly parts.** At minimum, model/review the observed insertion concept: transverse shaft through the appropriate aligned link/frame eyes, distinct shoulder/collar(s), and exposed outer cap. Identify each station from the model's local axes before assigning hardware to A/B/C. Do not merge a cross shaft into a planar bar or assume the E-joint stack applies to these joints.
5. **Do not freeze fork topology from these frames.** Record a potential split-end/clevis morphology as unresolved. Resolve it with a matched axial/rear source view or treat it as an explicitly labeled engineering assumption after checking that the neighboring axial layers and shaft can be assembled.
6. **Keep the moving torso/output connection on the existing torso body.** The broad slotted side plate and round central interface are visible; exact plate attachment, clevis shape, and bearing layers are hidden. Do not add independent E–D hardware or alter the center-frame load path based only on the ghosted frames.

No metric dimension, fit, material, bearing plane, shaft length, or fastening method is recovered from these perspective views.
