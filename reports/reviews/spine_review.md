## Prioritized findings

1. **P1 — Two independent crank connecting rods occupy the same material at G.** `src/build_robot.py:76-86` puts both `Chain1_L2_Side1` and `Chain2_L2_Side1` in y=38.7 mm (mirrored pair at −38.7), with identical 7 mm wide, 2 mm thick ends and 4.05 mm bores. Their end profiles each contain the disk of radius 3.5 mm centered on G; therefore they share at least the annular volume π(3.5²−2.025²)×2 = **51.204 mm³ per side**, irrespective of angle. The native XML confirms the common y placement and shared source. A single bearing layer at G cannot realize two independently rotating rods. Allocate distinct layers or a proper fork arrangement and update both B stacks and crank pin span. Do not exempt the overlapping polymer as intended bearing contact.

2. **P1 — The new input shaft does not fit either crank's O bore.** `src/build_robot.py:84` gives both crank holes 2.05 mm; lines 109–110 put a 3.99 mm through-shaft on O through both crank planes. Saved `SPN_CRANK_Bores` has radius 1.025 mm and `INPUT_SHAFT_D3p99_L110_Section` radius 1.995 mm. This produces **18.406 mm³ nominal overlap per crank**. Give O and G separate interface definitions and model a credible torque-locking crank hub for the selected shaft.

3. **P1 — The slider shoe overlaps its C bearing and connecting rod.** `src/build_robot.py:81,89,94-104` places the L4 link/bearing at y=45.1±1 mm and the 5 mm deep shoe at y=42.6…47.6 mm on the positive side. The shoe's C bore is only 2.12 mm while the bearing OD is 4 mm; the shoe also occupies the L4 material around that bore. The underside channel does not remove material around C (its top is z=−2.71 mm, while C is z=0). Separate the shoe from the rod stack, or provide a correctly dimensioned bearing/pin mounting recess and full joint stack. Mirror the resolved geometry rather than treating this as allowed contact.

4. **P1 — Scripted output rotation is not backed by a physical torso torque path.** `src/build_robot.py:82-83,121-128,185-190` rotates each cross-frame and cheek from beta, but the E–D output rocker has no rigid connection to either torso cheek. Its E end is bearing-mounted on a free 1.99 mm pin. On the positive side the E pin ends at y=53 mm; the cheek occupies y=52.5…55.5 with a 4.05 mm bore and no cheek bearing or rigid hub. Sharing a pivot does not transmit rocker torque. The cross-frame ends at y=51, leaving a 1.5 mm gap to the cheek, and their modeled screw holes have different axes. Add an explicit rocker-to-torso rigid connection and supported shaft/fork, then demonstrate the corresponding assembly load path. Source output-stage dimensions may remain declared assumptions.

5. **P1 — Several fixed assemblies are floating.** The rail occupies y=43.6…46.6 while its lumbar wall ends at y=30.75 (`src/build_robot.py:69-71,90-107`): there is **12.85 mm free space** and no rail bracket or connecting fastener. Stops are also separately placed without support. The motor saddles are z=−29…−27 while the lumbar floor ends at z=−36.5, with no posts across the 7.5 mm gap. Controller posts start at z=14 inside the chassis width without a supporting deck. Add explicit brackets/posts, retained rail engagement, and fastening paths; grouping these objects under Chassis/Actuators does not ground them. Verify sections after adding them, because new supports can cross moving links.

6. **P2 — 1:1 gear kinematics are sensible, but transmission integration remains provisional.** Equal module-1, 22-tooth gears at 22 mm centers and opposite signed rotations are consistent with an external 1:1 spur stage. The half-tooth offset is plausible for the modeled equal even-tooth gears. However, both gears have plain 4.05 mm bores, no torque adapters/keys/clamp hubs or axial capture, and no modeled motor-to-pinion shaft. The input shaft similarly has no positive axial capture. These are explicit physical interfaces to resolve, not evidence that the spur stage is mechanically integrated. Keep supplier output/horn geometry unknown until sourced; candidate adapter envelopes can be designed and labeled meanwhile. Check mesh and backlash through the actual sampled geometry.

7. **P2 — Parameter/report contracts can silently diverge.** `src/kinematics.py:22-29` ignores L7–L11 and guide travel keys, always using L2–L6 for both chains; validate the equality aliases or consume the declared chain lengths. The builder hard-codes rail 38 mm despite `guide_rail_length_mm=42`, shoe height 9 despite `carriage_height_mm=6`, motor y=15, and several other declared dimensions. `guide_min/max=±48` and `guide_stroke_min/max=±32` are ambiguous global limits rather than per-guide usable strokes. Replace duplicate/dead fields with one interface schema and derive CAD/travel checks from it. `src/verify_kinematics.py` emits output-range rows as `[both minima, both maxima]` with no ordering label: **per-chain ranges are chain 1 [−25.3944,+10.4176]°, chain 2 [−9.09958,+26.7125]°**. Label the axes or emit a keyed object. The report computes neutral relative to the first sample rather than `neutral_phase_rad`; these currently coincide but will diverge after a neutral-phase edit.
